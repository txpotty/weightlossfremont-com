    Understanding Low Desire in San Mateo

    Low sexual desire is one of the most common sexual health concerns women report. When it is persistent, causes distress and is not explained by another condition or medication, it may be diagnosed as hypoactive sexual desire disorder, or HSDD. Causes usually overlap. SSRIs, birth control, perimenopause, thyroid issues, pain with sex, sleep and relationship stress all play roles. A good evaluation looks at each.

    Low Libido Treatment for Women

    Treatment starts with finding what changed. Your provider reviews medications known to lower desire, asks about menopause symptoms, thyroid history and pain with sex, and looks at sleep and stress. Sometimes the answer is small. Treating vaginal dryness or adjusting an SSRI with your prescriber can make a real difference before any new medication is added. Medication options follow if needed.

    Flibanserin for Women in San Mateo

    Flibanserin, sold as Addyi, is FDA-approved for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized HSDD. The dose is one 100 mg tablet each night at bedtime, and it works on serotonin and dopamine rather than hormones. Results build slowly. If nothing has improved by about eight weeks, it is typically stopped. Alcohol timing matters with flibanserin.

    PT-141 for Women

    PT-141, or bremelanotide, is FDA-approved as Vyleesi for premenopausal women with acquired, generalized HSDD. It is a 1.75 mg injection under the skin of the belly or thigh, given at least 45 minutes before sex, and it acts on melanocortin receptors in the brain linked to desire. Expect some nausea early. The limits are one dose per day and eight per month. Compounded versions are not FDA-approved.

    Oxytocin Therapy in San Mateo

    Oxytocin is a hormone involved in bonding, touch and arousal. Some providers prescribe compounded oxytocin as a nasal spray or dissolving troche, usually used shortly before intimacy, to support arousal and connection. It is an off-label use. Compounded oxytocin is not FDA-approved for this purpose, research is limited and results vary widely. Your provider explains the evidence honestly.

    How a Provider Chooses Between Options

    Menopausal status comes first. Both FDA-approved drugs are labeled for premenopausal women, which means postmenopausal women often get a different plan. Then comes routine. Regular drinkers often lean toward PT-141, women with blood pressure issues toward flibanserin, and liver function shapes the choice too. Your preferences count as well.

    Side Effects and Safety in San Mateo

    Flibanserin can cause dizziness, sleepiness, nausea and low blood pressure. Alcohol and CYP3A4-blocking drugs make those effects worse, which explains the bedtime dose and drinking rules. PT-141 causes nausea often. It also causes flushing and headache, briefly raises blood pressure and can darken skin with repeated use. Oxytocin can cause headache or nasal irritation.
